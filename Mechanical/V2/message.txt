What a Strong Conclusion Should Do

Right now the conclusion section is empty.

This is actually one of the most important parts of the paper.

A conclusion is NOT:

a summary of every section
a repetition of the introduction
“we worked hard and learned a lot”

Instead, a strong engineering conclusion should answer:

1. What was achieved?

Explain the final outcome of the project.

Example:

successful redesign for the 2026 rules
improved reliability
improved localization
faster response
modular/open-source robot
2. What were the most important engineering improvements?

Mention the major advances:

new wheels
localization
ball sensing fusion
distributed processing
goalkeeper algorithm
lighter chassis

This proves technical progression.

3. What engineering philosophy guided the project?

This is especially important for your team because accessibility/open-source is one of your core themes.

You should reinforce:

low-cost engineering
manufacturability
reproducibility
iterative development
open-source sharing
4. What did you learn or discover?

This is where you discuss:

tradeoffs
testing
iteration
practical engineering lessons

For example:

material choice mattered more than expected
localization reduced line exits significantly
sensor fusion improved robustness
mechanical vibration affected vision systems

This makes the paper feel like real engineering work rather than a showcase.

5. What comes next?

A good conclusion usually ends by mentioning future work.

Not too much detail — just enough to show continued development.

Examples:

improving localization accuracy
better path planning
machine vision expansion
more advanced team coordination
improved sensor fusion
What Your Conclusion SHOULD Feel Like

The tone should feel:

reflective
engineering-focused
forward-looking
confident but not exaggerated

It should feel like:

“Here is what we built, why it matters, what we learned, and where we are going next.”

Other Important Feedback About the TDP
1. The paper is unusually educational

This is both:

a strength
and a possible weakness

Some sections read more like tutorials/textbook explanations.

Example:

very detailed explanation of how boost converters work
extensive explanation of comparators
detailed semiconductor behavior

This is not necessarily bad, but sometimes it becomes longer than needed for a TDP.

Judges usually care more about:

YOUR implementation
YOUR decisions
YOUR testing
YOUR results

than about textbook theory.

Recommendation

Try to slightly reduce:

generic electronics explanations

and increase:

robot-specific results
testing outcomes
performance improvements
engineering tradeoffs

For example:
instead of spending many lines explaining how inductors work in general, you could add:

recharge time
measured current
kicking performance
reliability observations

That makes the paper feel more research-oriented.
